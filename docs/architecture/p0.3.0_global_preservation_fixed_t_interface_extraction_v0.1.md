### Closed membership representation and P0.2.7 equivalence

For \(a>0,H\ge0\), define the exact explicit one-sided function

\[
\Psi_a(c,H)=\max_{0\le s\le H}(cs-\tfrac12as^2)
=c s_* -\tfrac12a s_*^2,\qquad s_*=\min\{H,\max\{0,c/a\}\}.
\]

On \(y,w\ge0\), take \(\Theta_O=T_O^{\min}(y,w)\), the frozen P0.2.7
`abort.TOmin`, and set

\[
F_R^O=g_R-\Psi_C(c_R,\Theta_O),\quad
F_F^O=g_F-\Psi_B(c_F,\Theta_O),\quad
F_G^O=g_R+g_F-(c_R+c_F)\Theta_O.
\]

Then \(z\in\mathcal K_O\iff F_R^O,F_F^O,F_G^O\ge0\).
For completion, define

\[
D_T=W-y,\quad D_{\mathrm{phys}}=w^2/(2A)-\bar\delta_T,\quad
\mathcal P_T=\{D_T\ge D_{\mathrm{phys}},w\ge0\}.
\]

Only on \(\mathcal P_T\), use the finite frozen P0.2.7
\(\Theta_T=T_T^{\min}(D_T,w)\) (`completion.TTmin`) and set

\[
F_T^C=g_T-\Psi_C(c_T,\Theta_T),\qquad
z\in\mathcal K_T\iff (y,w)\in\mathcal P_T\ \land\ F_T^C\ge0.
\]

Outside \(\mathcal P_T\), completion membership is false; do not evaluate
\(\Psi\) at an artificial infinite completion time. An unavailable \(F_T^C\)
there is a diagnostic absence, not undefined membership.

P0.2.7 `abort.feasible` and `completion.feasible` are theoretically sufficient
membership tests on their supported finite-input pre-decision domain, by the
same Parts I–II equivalences. Its `isfinite(TTmin)` guard correctly excludes
physical nonrecoverability even with infinite opportunity. Negative gaps
already disable the relevant branch. `joint.dualCapable` still needs the base
set check for a global preservation-state predicate, especially outside the
pre-decision domain where P0.2.7 throws rather than classifies.

**Recommendation: Option 2, a future thin closed-membership evaluator**:

```matlab
membership = avlc.analysis.evaluateGlobalPreservationMembership(scenario,z);
```

Proposed outputs: `baseAdmissible`, `abortMember`, `completionMember`,
`preservationMember`, physical-recoverability flag, four closed margins,
domain/defined/reason metadata and theory provenance. It should reuse
Scenario validation and frozen execution times, then add only the Part-VI
\(\Psi/F\) representation. No opportunity, frontier, or minimum-time formula
should be copied or altered. Optional consumption of a matching existing
`cap` may be considered later; coherent provenance must be checked or required.

This is an alternative representation of the same capability sets, not a
second theory. Option 1 remains an independent regression reference. Require
equivalence tests at zero time, zero gaps, discriminant equality, finite/Inf
opportunity transitions, both completion branches and physical-recoverability
equality. Do not implement this evaluator in P0.3.0.

Closed \(F\) margins avoid finite/Inf clock transitions and cancellation in
small positive opportunity roots, making them the preferred numerical
representation for GF3/horizon state constraints. They are not a guarantee
against floating-point overflow or cancellation near zero margins. Arithmetic
failure must be reported; it cannot be silently treated as nonmembership.
No tolerance may redefine the mathematical closed set.

For valid finite states outside the base set, `preservationMember=false` is a
defined answer. When \(y<0\) or \(w<0\), do not call P0.2.7 outside its domain:
individual capability predicates can be unavailable while the base check
already proves nonmembership in the preservation set. Malformed inputs and
unsupported arithmetic are distinct from this physical nonmembership.

## C. Lateral endpoint interface

Master §§91–103, including P5b-L1/P5b-T1, give all needed formulas:

\[
\alpha(t)=\frac{A+a_y(t)}{2A}\in[0,1],\quad
\rho(t)=A\int_0^t\alpha(s)\,ds,\quad
\sigma(t)=A\int_0^t(1-\alpha(s))\,ds.
\]

Thus \(\rho+\sigma=AT\). These are positive and negative acceleration
allocations, measured in velocity units; \(\rho/A\) is a normalized allocation
duration, not a longitudinal moment or a lateral position.

\[
\rho_-(T)=\max\{0,(AT-w_0)/2\},\quad
\rho\in[\rho_-(T),AT],\quad\sigma=AT-\rho,\quad
w_T=w_0+2\rho-AT.
\]

\[
\bar y_T=y_0+w_0T+2T\rho-\tfrac12AT^2-\rho^2/A,
\qquad \beta=\frac2A\int_{\mathcal P}\sigma\,d\rho,
\qquad y_T=\bar y_T-\beta.
\]

With \(x_+=\max\{x,0\}\),

\[
\beta_{\max}(T,\rho)=\frac2A\left[
\rho(AT-\rho)-\tfrac12(AT-\rho-w_0)_+^2\right],\quad
0\le\beta\le\beta_{\max}.
\]

\[
y^+=\bar y_T,\qquad y^-=\bar y_T-\beta_{\max},\qquad
\boxed{\mathcal R_y(T,\rho)=[y^-,y^+]\text{ exactly}.}
\]

Intermediate \(\beta\) values are attainable, not merely bounded: §98 supplies
the convex interpolation of two progressive extremal controls. For
\(\beta_{\max}>0\), its interpolation parameter is
\(\lambda=\beta/\beta_{\max}\); for zero width use \(\lambda=0\).
Progressivity and \(y_0\ge0\) imply \(y(t)\ge0\). No longitudinal or
capability constraint is included in this lateral interval.

Proposed pure API (design only):

```matlab
reach = avlc.analysis.evaluateLateralEndpointReachability(scenario,z0,T,rho);
```

The Scenario supplies \(A\); the shorter `(z0,T,rho)` alone would omit a
required parameter because the frozen eight-field `z0` contains no \(A\).
Only `z0.y/z0.w` and \(A\) are mathematically consumed. Inputs require finite
scalars, \(A>0,y_0,w_0,T\ge0\) and exact allocation-domain membership.
Outputs should expose `rhoMin/rhoMax`, `sigma`, `wTerminal`, `barY`,
`betaMax`, `yMinus/yPlus`, `defined/reason` and exact-explicit provenance.
At \(T=0\): \(\rho=\sigma=\beta_{\max}=0\), \(w_T=w_0\),
\(y^-=y^+=y_0\). No sampling or maximization is needed.

## D. Longitudinal moment interface

Master §§107–112,125:

\[
\mu(t)=\frac{a_x(t)+B}{K}\in[0,1],\quad
\eta=\int_0^T\mu(s)\,ds,\quad
\zeta=\int_0^T(T-s)\mu(s)\,ds.
\]

Units: \(\eta\) is time; \(\zeta\) is time squared. The raw terminal lens is

\[
\mathcal M_x(T)=\{(\eta,\zeta):0\le\eta\le T,
\quad\eta^2/2\le\zeta\le T\eta-\eta^2/2\}.
\]

The true safe set is instead defined by

\[
(\eta,\zeta)\in\mathcal M_{x,\mathrm{safe}}(T)
\iff \exists\text{ measurable }a_x\in[-B,C]
\text{ with these moments and }
g_R(t),g_F(t),g_T(t)\ge0\quad\forall t\in[0,T].
\]

In general \(\mathcal M_{x,\mathrm{safe}}(T)\subseteq\mathcal M_x(T)\),
and they are **not interchangeable**. Equality can occur for particular
nonbinding scenes; it is not a general theorem. Endpoint gaps alone do not
certify all-prefix safety. Distinct controls can share both terminal moments.

The useful audit representation is

\[
V(t)=\int_0^t a_x(s)ds,\quad X(t)=\int_0^t V(s)ds,\quad X(0)=V(0)=0,
\]

\[
L_R=c_{R0}t-g_{R0},\quad L_T=c_{T0}t-g_{T0},\quad
U_F=g_{F0}-c_{F0}t,\quad\max\{L_R,L_T\}\le X(t)\le U_F.
\]

Terminal transform: \(v=K\eta-BT\), \(x=K\zeta-BT^2/2\).
A future membership oracle should consume `(scenario,z0,T,moments,options)`
and return a scoped decision plus evidence, not a bare Boolean. The raw lens
is a necessary rejection filter, never its safe membership implementation.
At \(T=0\), moments are zero and safety is the initial three-gap check.

## E. Safe-moment computational decision

### Exact GF1-PC condition and conditional formulas

For each \(T>0\) and \(v\in[-BT,CT]\), §114 defines
\(\mathcal E_T(v)\) by an actual safe bounded-double-integrator trajectory
with terminal velocity \(v\). Sections 119–124 retain the necessary bounds

\[
\underline x_{\mathrm{safe}}=
\max\{x_0^-,x_R^-,x_T^-\},\qquad
\overline x_{\mathrm{safe}}=\min\{x_0^+,x_F^+\},
\]

where

\[
x_0^-=(v+BT)^2/(2K)-BT^2/2,\qquad
x_0^+=CT^2/2-(CT-v)^2/(2K),
\]

\[
x_j^-=c_{j0}T-g_{j0}+\Psi_C(v-c_{j0},T)\quad(j=R,T),\qquad
x_F^+=g_{F0}-c_{F0}T-\Psi_B(-c_{F0}-v,T).
\]

If a fixed-\(v\) safe section is nonempty, its exact supports give that
interval (§123). The additional **GF1-PC** is precisely the converse
projection/nonemptiness implication (§124):

\[
\underline x_{\mathrm{safe}}(T,v)\le\overline x_{\mathrm{safe}}(T,v)
\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathcal E_T(v)\ne\varnothing.
\]

It must hold on the actual requested scene/horizon/terminal-velocity domain,
with the same reduced constant-velocity traffic, affine walls, acceleration
box and fixed-terminal-velocity conditions. A general-purpose envelope
oracle would need it for every queried \(T,v\). This is an extra condition,
not a conclusion from already using the reduced scene, convexity, nonempty
support formulas, or initial positive gap slack. The Master identifies the
unproved regular-slack terminal-velocity perturbation as the missing step;
it supplies no stronger independently established sufficient checklist that
software may substitute for GF1-PC.

Only if this additional condition is established or explicitly accepted as
a software-only assumption may §126 be used as an iff membership test:

\[
\underline\zeta_{\mathrm{safe}}=
\max\{\eta^2/2,\phi_R,\phi_T\},\qquad
\overline\zeta_{\mathrm{safe}}=
\min\{T\eta-\eta^2/2,\phi_F\},
\]

with \(v=K\eta-BT\),
\(\phi_j=(x_j^-+BT^2/2)/K\) for \(j=R,T\), and
\(\phi_F=(x_F^++BT^2/2)/K\). Then
\(0\le\eta\le T\) and
\(\underline\zeta_{\mathrm{safe}}\le\zeta\le\overline\zeta_{\mathrm{safe}}\)
describe the conditional explicit safe set.

**GF1-PC is not accepted in this design.** The user requested comparison,
not assumption activation. Envelope overlap remains necessary information;
it cannot establish sufficiency without that condition. No core dependency
or default option is allowed to activate it implicitly.

**Recommend Route C**, backed by the exact existence-defined contract of A;
A is the theorem specification, not a second computational route selection.
Do not implement B as the default. Candidate skeletons are not needed for C.

First use a piecewise-constant \(a_x\) transcription. Candidate search may
use LP with nodal wall constraints or an exchange procedure; independently
check each resulting segment over its entire duration before accepting a
witness. On a segment of length \(h\), with constant \(a_i\),

\[
X_i(s)=X_i+V_i s+\tfrac12 a_i s^2,\quad V_i(s)=V_i+a_i s,
\qquad0\le s\le h.
\]

Each signed wall gap is quadratic. Its minimum is tested at segment ends
and any interior derivative-zero point, separately for R, T and F; this
avoids mistaking nodal safety for path safety. Moment equalities and the
acceleration box also require independent verification. These elementary
segment identities propose a witness checker, not a new equivalence theorem
for the full measurable-control set.

At fixed mesh the verified piecewise-constant family is a subset of the true
safe-control family. Restricted-family failure, LP infeasibility, exhausted
search and solver termination do **not** prove that the true safe set is
empty. Mesh refinement is experimental evidence; no convergence/equivalence
at boundary-only solutions is claimed. A separate outer relaxation or a
control-independent necessary bound may provide exclusion evidence, but its
validity for all measurable controls must be established separately.

The future default contract should not require an optimization toolbox for
evaluating explicit maps or verifying supplied witnesses. Automatic LP search
is an optional backend that would require Optimization Toolbox when using
MathWorks `linprog`; no toolbox or external solver is added now. This API fact
was checked in the official [linprog documentation](https://www.mathworks.com/help/optim/ug/linprog.html)
and [Optimization Toolbox documentation](https://www.mathworks.com/help/optim/).
Missing search capability returns oracle-unavailable/unknown, not false.

Route C can support an auditable numerical estimate or bracket for
\(\tau_{\mathrm{dual}}^*\). A checked preserving witness supplies a lower
bound. An upper bound needs valid global exclusion evidence, not the first
failed numerical search. Without such evidence or a completeness result,
the software cannot claim the exact global horizon. Floating-point residual
tolerances alone do not turn a certificate into a mathematical proof.

## F. Fixed-T interface

### Endpoint state and reuse of terminal capability geometry

For \(T>0\) and \(q=(\rho,\eta,\zeta)\), Master §§137–138 gives

\[
w_T=w_0+2\rho-AT,
\]

\[
c_F^T=c_{F0}-BT+K\eta,\quad
c_R^T=c_{R0}+BT-K\eta,\quad
c_T^T=c_{T0}+BT-K\eta,
\]

\[
g_F^T=g_{F0}-c_{F0}T+BT^2/2-K\zeta,
\]
\[
g_R^T=g_{R0}-c_{R0}T-BT^2/2+K\zeta,\quad
g_T^T=g_{T0}-c_{T0}T-BT^2/2+K\zeta.
\]

The triple determines terminal \(w\), all three closing speeds and all three
gaps, and therefore every terminal frontier parameter. It does **not** select
a unique \(y_T\): \(y_T\in[y^-,y^+]\), equivalently a choice of \(\beta\),
completes the eight-dimensional reduced terminal state. It does not identify
a unique trajectory either. Neither full vehicle states nor geometry need
to be reintroduced into this endpoint map.

**Reuse the frozen `evaluateCapability` to obtain the terminal corridor.**
Construct a reduced probe with the seven terminal quantities above and a
documented nonnegative reference `y` (for example `z0.y`). Its finite

quantities and Scenario constants, not on the probe's `y`. Its capability
Booleans and `TTmin` refer to that probe and must **not** be interpreted as
the selected terminal state's capability. Probe values may be discarded
after extracting certified chart geometry. This adapts the existing API
without copying opportunity/frontier formulas or changing P0.2.7.

Then use

\[
H_O^T=H_O(g_R^T,c_R^T,g_F^T,c_F^T),\quad
H_T^T=H_T(g_T^T,c_T^T),
\]

\[
Y_O^T=A(H_O^T)^2/4-H_O^T w_T/2-w_T^2/(4A),
\]

\[
D_+^T=A(H_T^T)^2/4+H_T^T w_T/2-w_T^2/(4A),\quad
D_-^T=-A(H_T^T)^2/4+H_T^T w_T/2+w_T^2/(4A),
\]

\[
D_{\mathrm{phys}}^T=w_T^2/(2A)-\bar\delta_T,\quad
Y_{\mathrm{in}}^T=W-D_+^T,\quad
Y_{\mathrm{out}}^T=W-\max\{D_{\mathrm{phys}}^T,D_-^T\},
\]

\[
Y_{\mathrm{fwd}}^T=\min\{Y_O^T,Y_{\mathrm{out}}^T\},\qquad
\text{terminal dual corridor }[Y_{\mathrm{in}}^T,Y_{\mathrm{fwd}}^T].
\]

These are extracted formulas for review, **not instructions to duplicate them
in a future solver**. A candidate requires safe terminal gaps, finite
opportunities/frontiers and \(H_T^T\ge w_T/A\) to use this corridor. A proven
failure of the latter is a rejection of that endpoint's completion capability,
not evidence that every endpoint triple fails. Inf/NaN geometry cannot be
repaired by replacing it with a large finite number. Outside the finite
chart the Part-IX reduction is unsupported; use global membership for
pointwise questions, not manufactured finite reserves.

Once an intersection point is selected, form the actual `zT` with that
`yTerminal` and reevaluate frozen capability for witness validation. The
probe adapter needs explicit independence/equivalence tests before use.

### P5b-Lift domain certificate — mandatory

Master B1–B4 assumes all of the following, not merely terminal contact:

1. \(\Delta_F(0),\Delta_R(0),\Delta_T(0)>0\), with
   \(\Delta_F=\sqrt{c_F^2-2Bg_F}\) and
   \(\Delta_j=\sqrt{c_j^2-2Cg_j}\), \(j=R,T\).
2. Relevant initial quadratic opportunity clocks are finite. In this
   reduced implementation, certify F, R and T individually, including
   positive closing speeds and strictly positive discriminants, not only
   finiteness of their minimum. \(T_G\) is finite under item 3 and finite gaps.
3. \(c_{O0}=c_{R0}+c_{F0}>0\).
4. \(y_0\ge0,w_0\ge0\).
5. Measurable admissible controls with \(a_x\in[-B,C]\),
   \(a_y\in[-A,A]\) almost everywhere, obeying the reduced dynamics.
6. \(w(t)\ge0\) for the entire prefix.
7. Base admissibility is **exactly** Part VI's reduced domain, with all three
   gaps nonnegative for every prefix time, and no hidden transient constraint.

The initial data checks alone are not a path certificate. Item 7's
longitudinal path safety is supplied by true safe-moment membership or a
verified safe witness; item 6 by exact lateral reachability/construction.
Initial preservation membership should be reported explicitly. If it fails,
no preserving prefix including time zero can exist. Strict positivity of
every initial reserve is not an extra stated P5b-Lift hypothesis.

Under precisely these assumptions the Master gives

\[
\big(r_O(T),\ell_T(T),r_T(T)\ge0\big)
\iff
\big(r_O(t),\ell_T(t),r_T(t)\ge0\quad\forall t\in[0,T]\big).
\]

Strict discriminants cannot collapse forwards; finite opportunities and
\(p\)-coordinate monotonicity, together with terminal feasibility, maintain
the valid reserve domain. Audited switching keeps the reserves continuous
and piecewise absolutely continuous. These are the Master's reasons for
the bridge; the software does not assume that an arbitrary terminal-safe
state implies a safe prefix. No extension to infinite charts or P6 follows.
Terminal \(w_T=0\) or \(p_T=0\) must not be rejected merely for failing
P0.2.9's stricter local regular chart: Lift and GF2 have distinct domains.

### Exact finite-chart fixed-T statement

Assuming P5b-Lift, Theorem P5b-T3 (§141) states that preservation through
\(T\) exists iff some

\[
\rho\in[\rho_-(T),AT],\qquad
(\eta,\zeta)\in\mathcal M_{x,\mathrm{safe}}(T)
\]

satisfies

\[
\boxed{[y^-(T,\rho),y^+(T,\rho)]\cap
[Y_{\mathrm{in}}^T,Y_{\mathrm{fwd}}^T]\ne\varnothing.}
\]

Let \(L=Y_{\mathrm{in}}^T,U=Y_{\mathrm{fwd}}^T\). Equivalently,

\[
M_{\mathrm{corr}}=U-L,\quad M_{\mathrm{dead}}=y^+-L,\quad
M_{\mathrm{fwd}}=U-y^-,\qquad
\min\{M_{\mathrm{corr}},M_{\mathrm{dead}},M_{\mathrm{fwd}}\}\ge0
\]

for some valid triple. The exact existential domain is
\(\mathcal Q_T^{\mathrm{core}}\), not a prescribed optimizer skeleton.
If useful for reporting, §144 gives

\[
m=(L+U)/2,\qquad
\Phi_{\mathrm{dual}}=(U-L)/2-\operatorname{dist}(m,[y^-,y^+]).
\]

The core is \(\exists q\in\mathcal Q_T^{\mathrm{core}}:
\Phi_{\mathrm{dual}}(q)\ge0\). No attained scalar maximum is required.
A witness can select any point in the intersection and set
\(\beta=\bar y_T-y_T\); it need not maximize any margin.

These three quantities are **fixed-T endpoint-feasibility margins only**.
Zero means endpoint contact. They are not dynamic first-loss theorems,
terminal obstruction classes, maneuver-freedom loss mechanisms or controller
objectives. Later loss diagnostics need the compatible GF2/domain analysis
and the attained-horizon context of GF3; contact alone proves no loss.

| `domain` | Initial base/capability membership, discriminants, initial clock finiteness, \(c_{O0}\), reduced-model assumptions, path-certificate references, defined/failed checks |
| `provenance` | Master hash, baseline revision, exact/conditional/numerical basis, oracle type and assumptions; GF1-PC inactive by default |
| `endpoint` | Witness triple if found, selected \(y_T,\beta\), actual reduced `zT`, reachable interval, terminal corridor, three margins |
| `safeMomentEvidence` | Moments, control representation, mesh, exact segment propagation, whole-segment extrema and minimum gaps, residual/error bounds |
| `lateralEvidence` | Allocation domain, extremal/interpolated progressive construction, interval intersection and terminal checks |
| `numerical` | Candidate found/not found, mesh/solver settings, tolerances, residuals, termination cause and error-control level |
| `exclusionEvidence` | A valid all-control necessary-bound contradiction or rigorous outer certificate, with its scope, if one exists |

Truth rules:

- `CERTIFIED_FEASIBLE`: a theorem-valid witness is certified, including
  whole-prefix safety and the Lift assumptions when the endpoint theorem
  is used. It proves existence, not optimality. The witness also proves
  global existence when verified against the global model; do not confuse
  that implication with equivalence of the two domains.
- `CERTIFIED_INFEASIBLE`: requires a valid exclusion covering **all** controls
  and endpoint coordinates in the reported scope. One failed candidate,
  an infeasible restricted LP, or a negative margin at one triple is insufficient.
  A finite-chart exclusion cannot be relabeled global without a separate
  coverage argument. Initial global preservation nonmembership is a direct
  exclusion for any horizon including zero.
- `UNKNOWN`: assumptions supported but the exact oracle is unavailable,
  the numerical search is incomplete, or arithmetic cannot certify a sign.
  An ordinary floating-point witness with tolerance checks is retained as
  numerical evidence, with theorem-level `feasible=NaN` until a justified
  error bound or rigorous certificate supports the claim. This prevents
  a numerical approximation from acquiring exact truth by a status label.
- `UNDEFINED`: the requested theorem reduction/model/domain is unsupported,
  for example a finite-atlas request at an initial infinite opportunity.
  It does not mean physically impossible. Malformed inputs should raise
  validation errors, not masquerade as a feasibility result.

Actual valid closed-boundary nonmembership is false, not undefined. The
finite-Lift certificate is not inherited from P0.2.9's `defined` flag; a
local GF2 `UNDEFINED` likewise cannot disprove global preservation.
No margins are filled with guessed finite replacements for NaN/Inf.
